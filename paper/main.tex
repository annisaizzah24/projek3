\begin{abstract}
The distribution of social assistance relies on consistent and traceable citizen eligibility assessments, whereas manual or decentralized examinations risk producing ununiform and uncorrectable evaluations. This study evaluates the Sociovault prototype, a Flask-based web application running a three-stage rule engine for a single assistance program: data completeness checks, four-criterion evaluations, and final officer confirmation. This research aims to test whether the system's recommendations and rationales follow defined rules, and whether decisions can be traced and corrected. The method used is rule compliance testing against a rule matrix with 60 synthetic cases (Test 1) along with boundary value analysis and duplicate verification (Test 2). The results show label compliance of 100\% and rationale compliance of 100\% across all evaluated deterministic scenarios. Furthermore, the evaluation identifies an architectural boundary: while citizen socioeconomic attributes can be updated via an administrative appeal workflow, the engine enforces strict single-verification immutability, requiring formal re-evaluation mechanisms for updated profiles.

\keywords{Sociovault \and social assistance \and eligibility verification \and rule engine \and decision traceability \and rule compliance}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Social assistance programs serve as an essential government mechanism to protect vulnerable populations and alleviate poverty~\cite{uu11_2009}. In developing countries, including Indonesia, targeting welfare recipients remains a persistent challenge characterized by exclusion errors (ineligible households receiving aid) and inclusion errors (impoverished households excluded from benefits)~\cite{alatas2012targeting,hanna2018universal}. Under the national Electronic-Based Government System framework (\textit{Sistem Pemerintahan Berbasis Elektronik} / SPBE)~\cite{perpres95_2018}, public service delivery must be transformed into integrated, objective, and auditable digital processes. Examining applicant data is therefore a vital operational step to ensure that eligibility recommendations are grounded in verifiable household parameters and predefined regulatory criteria rather than discretionary manual judgments.

Several systemic risks must be addressed during automated eligibility assessments, including incomplete applicant records, duplicate claims across overlapping assistance programs, and determinations delivered without clear, verifiable explanations~\cite{lindert2020sourcebook,busuioc2021accountable}. Incomplete records frequently cause automated engines to either silently drop applicants or issue erroneous rejections, whereas duplicate records distort budgetary allocation and violate fairness principles. Crucially, algorithmic decisions rendered without granular, criteria-level rationales hinder caseworkers and citizens from understanding why assistance was denied and prevent the timely correction of erroneous data. In public assistance administration, accountability requires that every decision can be audited against its underlying policy rules~\cite{sunny2025neuro}.

A viable technological approach is rule-based eligibility evaluation, which formalizes policy regulations into explicit, deterministic criteria~\cite{lopez2022envisioning}. To maintain operational integrity, rule engines must be supported by decision traceability---the ability to reconstruct the exact relationship between applicant input parameters, applied threshold criteria, and generated recommendations. Traceability requires maintaining comprehensive audit logs of all inspection steps, recording parameter versions, and systematically isolating incomplete or ambiguous cases for human inspection (\texttt{perlu\_ditinjau}) rather than prematurely rejecting vulnerable citizens without explicit evidentiary cause~\cite{cobbe2021reviewable}.

To investigate these operational requirements, this study evaluates rule compliance and decision traceability in a social assistance eligibility prototype called \textbf{Sociovault}. As a concrete evaluation benchmark, the study configures a representative social assistance scheme (designated as \textbf{Program A}), modeled upon Indonesia's conditional cash assistance framework (\textit{Program Keluarga Harapan} / PKH). Eligibility under this benchmark is governed by four deterministic criteria: monthly income $\le$ Rp 2,000,000, dependent count $\ge$ 2, total asset value $\le$ Rp 15,000,000, and welfare decile $\le$ 3. These threshold parameters reflect statutory targeting guidelines established under Ministry of Social Affairs regulations (DTKS) and national poverty line indicators, which prioritize impoverished households in the bottom 30\% welfare percentiles (Deciles 1--3) supporting multiple family dependents~\cite{permensos3_2021}. Specifically, this research addresses two primary Research Questions (RQs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} \textit{To what extent does the prototype's multi-stage rule engine adhere to defined program eligibility criteria and boundary conditions without making premature automated decisions on incomplete or anomalous applications?}
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} \textit{Can the decision rationale generated by the system be traced and audited at the per-criterion level, and how does the prototype handle data correction and re-evaluation when applicant profiles are updated?}
\end{itemize}

The evaluation examines 60 synthetic cases across standard and edge-case profiles (Test 1), boundary threshold values, and duplicate submission scenarios (Test 2). The findings demonstrate deterministic rule and rationale compliance while uncovering critical architectural constraints regarding post-decision immutability and citizen data re-evaluation.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Rule-Based Eligibility and Transforming Policies into Decision Models}

Research on social service eligibility automation emphasizes that eligibility rules are often embedded in complex policy documents, making them difficult to translate into executable logic. Lopez et al. proposed a human-AI collaborative approach using natural language processing and knowledge graphs to transform eligibility policy documents into executable and interpretable decision models based on Decision Model and Notation (DMN) standards and Friendly Enough Expression Language (FEEL)~\cite{lopez2022envisioning}. This approach separates regulatory policy definitions from core application logic. 

Industry systems similarly advocate decoupling business rules into auditable decision engines to ensure regulatory audit readiness~\cite{progress_software}. In contrast, the Sociovault prototype adopts a lightweight architecture tailored for municipal administration: criteria parameters are stored in relational database tables (\texttt{help\_program}) and evaluated by a deterministic rule engine (\texttt{run\_verify} / \texttt{jalankan\_verifikasi} in Python). Consequently, verifying that this embedded engine strictly adheres to coded specifications is essential.

\subsection{Explainable and Auditable Decision Support for Public Services}

The literature on automated public assistance systems increasingly prioritizes auditability over generic model interpretability. Sunny demonstrated that explanations produced by automated eligibility systems often diverge from underlying legal statutes, proposing a neuro-symbolic framework that ties generated explanations to formal requirements ontologies and satisfiability solvers~\cite{sunny2025neuro}. Under this framework, public-sector algorithmic explanations must directly cite governing criteria so that determinations remain contestable by administrative officers and applicants~\cite{sunny2025neuro}.

While those studies investigate high-level legal argumentation for programs such as CalFresh in the United States, Sociovault operates at the operational administrative tier: its output is a structured, per-criterion evaluation detailing applicant values, comparative operators ($\le, \ge$), program thresholds, and individual pass/fail statuses. The primary evaluation concern for Sociovault is therefore whether these structured details faithfully represent input data and applied rules.

\subsection{Deduplication and Recipient Identity Matching}

Duplicate benefit disbursement has long presented an administrative vulnerability in public welfare. A landmark U.S. General Accounting Office (GAO) audit revealed that cross-jurisdictional duplicate welfare payments persisted primarily due to missing or unverified identification numbers and fragmented data formats~\cite{gao1978duplicate}. Historically, social service agencies implemented rule-based cross-matching: early state circulars employed exact name and birthdate matching to identify potential duplicate recipients for human case review~\cite{california1987match,california1994letter}.

Modern social protection architectures increasingly deploy automated entity resolution systems. The OpenG2P deduplication framework integrates multi-attribute fuzzy matching with biometric verifications to resolve duplicates across disparate identity sources~\cite{openg2p_dedup}. The openIMIS initiative designed dedicated deduplication modules incorporating probabilistic similarity algorithms and persistent audit logs~\cite{openimis2022algorithm}. Similar multi-tiered deduplication protocols have been codified in regional social welfare guidelines~\cite{dswd2020guidelines}, while system harmonization remains critical for humanitarian cash transfer programs~\cite{owino2020harmonising}.

In its current prototype version, Sociovault implements deterministic deduplication through unique constraints on National Identity Numbers (NIK) and unique citizen--program verification pairs. Rather than conducting probabilistic entity resolution, Sociovault enforces strict single-entry registration, making evaluation of its boundary behavior and error rejection critical.

\subsection{Identity Data Quality and Handling of Missing Data}

Incomplete or erroneous identity data is a primary source of targeting misclassification in public welfare registries~\cite{lindert2020sourcebook}. Established best practices in public assistance decision systems emphasize that missing mandatory attributes should be treated as pending cases requiring investigation rather than being summarily categorized as ineligible, preventing the unfair exclusion of entitled citizens~\cite{lindert2020sourcebook,permensos3_2021}. Sociovault aligns with this principle by routing missing mandatory fields and zero-income anomalies to a provisional ``\texttt{perlu\_ditinjau}'' (needs review) status rather than generating an automated negative determination.

\subsection{Rule Engine Execution and Rationale Logging}

The verification engine (\texttt{jalankan\_verifikasi}) executes three sequential stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Completeness Check:} If any required demographic or socioeconomic field is empty (\texttt{None} or blank string), the system halts standard assessment and assigns the status \texttt{perlu\_ditinjau}, appending the list of missing attributes to the rationale.
    \item \textbf{Economic Anomaly Detection:} If the reported income is exactly Rp 0, the engine flags the record as \texttt{perlu\_ditinjau}, generating an explicit directive that a field survey is required to verify real living conditions.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Criteria Assessment:} The four program criteria are evaluated: income $\le$ limit, dependents $\ge$ limit, assets $\le$ limit, and decile $\le$ limit. If all criteria are satisfied, the engine assigns \texttt{layak}; if one or more criteria fail, it assigns \texttt{tidak\_layak}.
\end{enumerate}

Every execution produces a structured \texttt{detail\_pengecekan} dictionary storing the applicant's input value, the program threshold, the evaluated comparison operator, and the pass/fail determination for each criterion.

\subsection{Heuristic Welfare Decile Estimation}

When an applicant lacks a pre-assigned government decile, Sociovault estimates their decile using a baseline formula in \texttt{engine.py}: per-capita income is computed as $\text{Income} / (\text{Dependents} + 1)$ and mapped across ten income brackets starting at Rp 600,000. This baseline is adjusted based on asset indicators (housing construction, electricity wattage, and vehicle ownership). In accordance with policy integrity, any pre-existing official decile recorded in the citizen profile strictly overrides the automated heuristic calculation.

\subsection{Duplicate Prevention and Data Correction Mechanism}

Deduplication operates across three architectural layers: (1) a unique database index on the citizen's NIK in the \texttt{warga} table, (2) a composite unique constraint on (\texttt{id\_warga}, \texttt{id\_program}) in the \texttt{verifikasi} table, and (3) an active pre-check in the verification engine that queries existing verification rows prior to processing.

\subsection{Evaluation Methodology and Synthesized Requirements}

To rigorously assess the prototype without compromising private citizen data, the evaluation utilizes synthetic cases with fully specified ground-truth conditions. The requirements evaluate deterministic compliance against coded program specifications rather than external policy validity.

\noindent\textbf{Duplicate Submission and Post-Correction Findings:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Initial Verification:} Running verification for an unverified citizen--program pair executes normally, creating a single record.
    \item \textit{Duplicate Verification Attempt:} Re-executing verification for the identical citizen and program is immediately rejected with \texttt{AlreadyVerifiedError}. Database row counts remain strictly unchanged.
    \item \textit{Post-Correction Re-evaluation (TC-DUP-02):} When an applicant's demographic profile is edited in the citizen registry (e.g., income updated from Rp 2,500,000 to Rp 1,500,000), subsequent verification attempts in \texttt{engine.py} are still rejected by the immutable duplicate barrier (\texttt{AlreadyVerifiedError}). This indicates that while the database correctly updates the citizen profile, the verification engine enforces strict immutability on prior program decisions, lacking a coupled re-evaluation or revocation workflow.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Test 3: Officer Decision Traceability Protocol Design}

To assess whether human officers can effectively interpret the criteria-level rationales generated in \texttt{detail\_pengecekan}, a structured 10-case evaluation protocol was designed. Under this protocol, officers are presented with verification outputs and asked to identify: (1) which specific criteria caused an applicant's rejection or review status, and (2) what corrective data attributes must be verified. While this protocol establishes a standardized methodology for human-in-the-loop auditability, empirical testing with active administrative officers was not conducted within this synthetic prototype phase and is reserved for future field deployments.

\section{Discussion: Citizen Decile Appeal Workflow and Re-Evaluation Bottlenecks}
\label{sec:decile_appeal_workflow}

To contextualize the data correction bottleneck identified in Test 2 (TC-DUP-02) and Table~\ref{tab:output3}, this section examines Sociovault's administrative appeal mechanism. Sociovault incorporates a citizen-facing decile appeal module (\nolinkurl{/pengajuan-desil}) designed to enable participatory data maintenance while safeguarding auditability.

\subsection{Decile Appeal Architecture and Procedure}

The appeal workflow operates across three sequential steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Status Inspection and Appeal Initiation (Fig.~\ref{fig:decile_step1}):} Citizens access their personal dashboard (\nolinkurl{/dashboard}) to review their current demographic record, welfare decile classification, and program history. When discrepancies arise, citizens initiate an appeal via ``\textit{Ajukan Perubahan Desil}.''
    \item \textbf{Socioeconomic Evidence Submission (Fig.~\ref{fig:decile_step2}):} Citizens submit updated factual attributes (number of dependents, declared monthly income, narrative justification) alongside verifiable physical evidence, such as photographic proof of housing conditions and poverty certificates (SKTM).
    \item \textbf{Administrative Audit and Verification Queue (Fig.~\ref{fig:decile_step3}):} Submitted appeals enter an administrative queue (\nolinkurl{/riwayat-pengajuan-desil}) labeled ``\textit{Menunggu Verifikasi Petugas}.'' Authorized village officers examine the submitted evidence, conduct on-site verification if warranted, and approve or reject the request with mandatory review notes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Decoupling: Profile Maintenance vs. Program Immutability}

While the decile appeal workflow successfully enables citizens and village officers to update master demographic records in the \texttt{warga} table (e.g., updating \texttt{warga.desil} and \texttt{warga.penghasilan}), it reveals an important architectural separation from the eligibility verification engine:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Master Profile Updating:} When an officer approves an appeal, the citizen's profile attributes are updated in the database with full audit logging (\nolinkurl{PengajuanDesil}).
    \item \textit{Engine Decision Immutability:} In \texttt{app/verifikasi/engine.py}, the rule engine enforces strict single-verification immutability to prevent duplicate disbursements for a given program cycle. Because prior verification records (\texttt{Verifikasi}) remain committed, running verification again for the same citizen and program pair triggers \texttt{AlreadyVerifiedError}.
\end{itemize}

This architectural separation explains the outcome of test case TC-DUP-02. The decile appeal workflow resolves data correction at the demographic registry level, but it does not automatically invalidate or recalculate existing program determinations. In municipal administration, this separation prevents unauthorized tampering with past disbursement rolls. However, to support legitimate re-examinations following verified economic changes, the system requires a formal, auditable re-evaluation protocol (such as administrative status revocation or multi-round verification lifecycles) rather than silent re-runs.

\section{Conclusion}

Based on the empirical evaluation of the Sociovault prototype, the findings are structured into four core conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Rule Compliance (RQ1):} Across 60 synthetic cases spanning eligible, ineligible, and inconclusive profiles, Sociovault's multi-stage rule engine achieved 100.0\% label compliance and 100.0\% rationale compliance against defined program criteria. The system produced zero false acceptances, zero false rejections, and zero premature automated determinations on review cases, correctly delegating ambiguous cases to administrative officers.
    \item \textbf{Traceability and Re-Evaluation Realities (RQ2):} The prototype provides complete per-criterion decision traceability via \texttt{detail\_pengecekan}, allowing officers to audit the exact criteria causing eligibility or review statuses. However, a critical architectural limitation exists regarding post-decision re-evaluation: while the decile appeal workflow enables citizens and officers to correct demographic profiles, the verification engine enforces strict single-verification immutability to prevent duplicate claims. Consequently, updated citizen profiles cannot be automatically re-evaluated under the same program without a formal administrative reset mechanism.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Boundaries:} These conclusions are subject to specific experimental constraints: evaluations were conducted using synthetic test cases for a single assistance program with simulation thresholds; official institutional data was not utilized; and the officer traceability protocol (Test 3) represents an evaluation design that has not yet been deployed in field trials.
    \item \textbf{Practical Contribution and Future Work:} Sociovault demonstrates that multi-stage rule engines combined with granular audit logging provide a transparent, accountable framework for municipal welfare assessment. Future work should focus on integrating formal decision-revocation lifecycles to support auditable re-evaluations, validating heuristic decile formulas against official poverty registries, and conducting empirical field studies with public assistance caseworkers.
\end{enumerate}
